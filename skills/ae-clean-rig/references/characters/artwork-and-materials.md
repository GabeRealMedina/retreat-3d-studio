# Artwork and Materials

## Preserve illustrated construction

- Measure the approved character silhouette, facial proportions, major contour landmarks, palette, overlap, and framing before rebuilding. Match those features in a native still before extending the rig.
- When the approved artwork uses bold graphic anatomy, retain its almond-shaped eyes, angular nose, expressive mouth, broad hands, flowing hair masses, and simplified continuous limbs. Keep any supplied likeness within that same degree of stylization.
- Build visible illustration components from native Shape Layers and editable Bézier paths. Use a continuous contour for a continuous form; separate details where independent motion or material control requires it. Do not impose a layer-count target.
- Cover internal rig boundaries through coherent geometry and overlap. Keep shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, and ankles connected. Preserve finger count, limb orientation, foreshortening, and depth order during deformation.
- Use foreground plants, oversized flowers, companions, and background shapes according to the approved composition. When adapting the canvas, reposition or extend the setting without stretching the character's anatomy.

## Textured volume

- Match the accepted grain treatment directly from the artwork or approved native render. Keep grain strength, size, color response, and tonal distribution separate from the shape geometry.
- Use native gradients and shading effects to preserve volume across hair, skin, clothing, petals, foliage, and fur. Configure Gradient Ramp and Noise when they reproduce the reference; discover the installed effects and their exact property names with `ae_catalog` before constructing a stack, and verify their behaviour in the current host.
- Attach texture and shading to the appropriate object or material group so they follow its transforms and deformation. Maintain continuous material appearance across connected parts and preserve the illustrated light direction.
- Expose independent controls for gradient texture and fine grain when both are used. Resolve their values from the accepted look; do not impose one numerical strength across every material or project.
- Constrain highlights and reflections to the intended surface. Animate a glasses reflection independently of the frame and face, with the lens boundary defining its visible extent.
- Verify material appearance in native rendered crops at the intended output scale. Check shadows and highlights, texture density, silhouette edges, material seams, and changing scale during motion. Correct crawling grain or excessive flicker without erasing the accepted texture.
