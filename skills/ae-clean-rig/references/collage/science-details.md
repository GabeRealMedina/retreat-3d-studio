# Scientific Details and Magnification

## Scientific Collage Details

Use this section only for scientific or explanatory artwork. Keep the illustration's meaning coherent and label uncertain or decorative content appropriately. Build diagrams from editable paths and independent marks where practical. Animate phase, write-on, or staggered emphasis in ways supported by the reference instead of adding random motion that changes the apparent claim.

For a dish containing small organisms or particles, use independently phased native dots and rounded capsules within a defined interior matte. At a roughly 600-pixel-wide prop, small particles can drift by about 2 pixels, while larger capsules can move by roughly 8 pixels horizontally and 6 vertically with restrained rotation. Tune count and scale to the visible shot rather than filling the dish with uniform noise.

Keep all motion within the inner boundary and beneath rim reflections. Vary phases and directions so the population does not pulse in synchrony. Increase movement speed through its time driver when more activity is needed, then recheck blur and containment; do not increase amplitude until objects escape the dish. This is illustrative animation, not a validated simulation of biological behavior.

Place annotations on clear paper, away from photo captions and optical elements. Move the whole label-and-arrow group when needed rather than hiding a collision with a larger shadow. Plan the camera to reveal the dish before its arrival is complete; a prop that fits in the final frame can still be cropped throughout its entrance.

For a research-paper insert, use an authentic permitted page when naming a real study. Preserve its hierarchy, margins, and focal text. Keep paper stock, page print, shadow, highlight, and annotation separate. The highlight belongs behind the letters, and the title must remain readable during the closest camera position. Do not replace documentary body text with evenly spaced decorative lines while claiming authenticity.

## Registered Magnification

Use this section only when a magnifying glass or optical reveal is required. Build one logical controller for the rim, handle, content aperture, highlight, and contact shadow. Keep the rim modest enough that the enlarged subject remains the focal point. Begin with moderate magnification around 1.3 to 1.6 times and tune against the actual artwork.

Sample the exact region underneath the lens, not a similar-looking duplicate placed by eye. Base and magnified content must share source time, diagram phase, and marks. Convert the lens center through the correct composition and surface coordinate spaces before computing the enlarged sample. A simple offset in an untransformed 2D canvas is not a general solution for a camera-facing precomposition nested inside other transforms.

Mask the enlarged content to the lens interior and keep its registration tied to the same controller as the rim. Magnify clean source artwork before applying final fine grain so the lens does not enlarge print texture into coarse fabric. Retain physically plausible material detail without doubling every noise layer inside the aperture.

Animate an entrance, a purposeful inspection path, a readable pause, and an exit. Use restrained lag or settling only where the reference supports it. Review the entire path for sampling slips, clipping, diagram discontinuity, and accidental overlap with captions. A correct mathematical sample is necessary but does not by itself make the lens visually convincing.
